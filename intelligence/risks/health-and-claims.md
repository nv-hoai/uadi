# Risk Card: Health and Persuasive Claims

**Trigger:** the product touches health, safety, well-being, medical advice or marketing claims.

## Rules

- State the product's limits plainly. An audio app, tracker or refill form is not a diagnosis, treatment or clinical service unless the facts say so.
- Do not invent clinical results, before/after outcomes, research, endorsements, press coverage, scarcity or social proof. Use only supplied evidence.
- Present ratings and testimonials with source, sample size and recency where available. Do not imply that a testimonial predicts the user's result.
- Put the trial length, price, cancellation rule and any material limitation next to the action, not only in fine print or after sign-up.
- For a health-adjacent flow, tell the user when to seek urgent help and what the form cannot do. Do not collect more health data than the task requires.
- Refuse or redirect sensitive, violent or unsafe requests with a brief reason; never silently generate a misleading result.

## Failure modes

- "Clinically proven" or "cures insomnia" without evidence.
- A countdown timer or "only 2 spots" invented for urgency.
- A medical intake form that implies diagnosis or collects full records unnecessarily.
- A testimonial presented as a guarantee.
