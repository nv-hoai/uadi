# Alternatives and Trade-offs

**Load when:** the problem is ambiguous, consequential or has materially different directions.

## Decision rules

1. Decide whether alternatives are warranted. A clear local fix needs no concept tournament; a new flow, ownership model or high-cost commitment does.
2. Frame the decision: objective, constraints, must-pass gates and who bears each trade-off.
3. Generate materially different directions, not three visual skins of one idea. Vary interaction model, information order, disclosure or responsibility.
4. Compare on the same evidence and criteria. Separate must-pass gates from preferences; mark unknown evidence rather than scoring it.
5. Write rationale that can be inspected: decision, evidence, rejected alternatives, why they lost, and what would change the choice.
6. Record the chosen direction and the unresolved risks. Do not present a preference as a proven outcome.

## Failure modes

- Comparing only cosmetic variants.
- Choosing by taste after the criteria were set.
- Hiding a serious trade-off in a footnote.
- Treating an assumption as evidence because it supports the preferred option.

## Validation

Can a reviewer reconstruct why this direction won, what was rejected and which evidence is still missing? If not, the rationale is not inspectable.
